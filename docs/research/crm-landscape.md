# Research log: the personal CRM baseline

**Accessed November 21, 2026.** Question: Which proven CRM patterns belong in a self-hosted, single-user relationship system, and what do they imply for data, retrieval, and operating boundaries?

GroktoCrawl ran three public searches: personal CRM contact and reminder workflows; CRM data modeling, identity and migration; and temporal graphs and transcript retrieval. Targeted crawls then read project documentation and source repositories. Standards and official implementation references inform feasibility. Product pages establish what their authors document, not usability or observed performance. No private examples, user benchmark, deployed compatibility test, or product-market-fit claim is included.

| Source | Finding retained | Implication for GRM |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Monica features](https://monicahq.com/en/features/) and [source](https://github.com/monicahq/monica) | Self-hostable personal CRM with contact methods, relationships, private notes, interactions and important dates. | Contacts plus notes are table stakes. The personal domain includes family and social context, not just work. |
| [PersonalCRM: Staying in touch](https://personalcrm.me/docs/guides/staying-in-touch) | A typed note may count as an interaction, while an observation need not reset cadence. A single note can reference several people. | Separate interaction, occurrence, and evidence types. Don’t use “last seen in search” as “last spoke.” |
| [Dex: Stay informed](https://getdex.com/docs/workflows/stay-informed) and [MCP tools](https://getdex.com/docs/ai/mcp-server) | Dex documents pending replies, contact resurfacing, LinkedIn title-change updates, and tools to manage contacts and follow-ups. | Neither event nudges nor MCP access is novel by itself. Test whether owning the record locally and answering across linked evidence is better. |
| [Meerkat CRM](https://github.com/fbuchner/meerkat-crm) | A self-hosted personal CRM with relationship graph visualization, multi-person activities, portable contact data, synchronization and webhook integrations. | Strong comparator. Graphs and webhooks already exist; evaluate agent-mediated evidence and time-aware answers rather than treating graph presence as differentiation. |
| [Twenty data model](https://docs.twenty.com/getting-started/core-concepts/data-model) and [CSV import prep](https://docs.twenty.com/user-guide/data-migration/how-tos/prepare-your-csv-files) | Typed objects separate people, companies, notes, tasks, and deals; import guidance uses stable keys for linking. | Borrow typed relations and careful imports, not deal stages. Source identifiers help replay safely; ambiguous person matches still need an explicit resolution path. |
| [vCard](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc6350.html) and [iCalendar](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc5545.html) | Standard interchange for contacts and calendar records, including partial birthday dates in vCard and calendar recurrence in iCalendar. | Make common data portable, but define a separate native history export and restore test. |
| [Graphiti](https://github.com/getzep/graphiti) and [RAG design: temporal retrieval](https://github.com/magnus919/agent-skills/blob/6ad3efdca7fb9cad1f506c4a31e5c6018f2ddbfc/rag-design/references/temporal-rag.md) | Graphiti documents temporally scoped facts, provenance and hybrid search. The design skill distinguishes timelines, bitemporal facts, and metric series. | Benchmark useful historical and path queries before choosing storage. Preserve originals and corrections instead of treating a current contact card as the only truth. |
| [PostgreSQL recursive queries](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/18/queries-with.html) and [range types](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/18/rangetypes.html) | Relational primitives can express traversal and time intervals. | A practical comparison baseline, not a claim about latency or a storage selection. |
| [MCP tools specification](https://modelcontextprotocol.io/specification/2026-07-28/server/tools) | A host can call named tools with structured input and receive tool results. | Compare what representative hosts actually preserve and surface. A tool result can advertise a queue, but scheduled delivery is a separate host capability to test. |

**Excluded:** Vendor comparison lists and migration pitches were discovery leads, not evidence about competitors. Broad RAG benchmarks were not used as proof a CRM graph will work. Public research did not establish import fidelity, accurate speaker attribution, or host interoperability; verify those on representative synthetic fixtures before approving architecture.

**Open evidence:** Collect ordinary questions from people who already keep relationship notes. Compare responses and failure behavior with a plain contact-and-notes baseline. Validate a correction backdated across a previous answer, interrupted import/restore, permissions after source deletion, and whether a queued update leads to a timely action rather than background noise.
