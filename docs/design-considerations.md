# Designing a personal CRM for one person

Discovery notes, November 21, 2026. These are design inputs, not an architecture decision or an implementation plan. See the [research log](research/crm-landscape.md) for what the comparison products and standards actually say.

## What a CRM preserves

At its core a CRM records people, their context, past interactions, and what should happen next. Business CRMs add opportunity stages, ownership, and work across teams. Those are useful for sales, but not the starting point here. Monica and PersonalCRM put the individual’s relationships, activities, and reminders first. Meerkat already offers a relationship graph, imports, and integrations. Dex already surfaces job-title changes, replies owed, and timely reasons to reconnect. GRM's hypothesis is narrower: can your existing assistant answer questions across your relationships and their history, with evidence, without sending you to another application?

## Feature needs and what they imply

| User job | What the record needs | Design consideration and first test |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Remember who someone is and how we know each other | People, organizations, identifiers, aliases, and typed relationships with properties, source, and dates | Distinguish a person you know from a person two steps away or merely being watched. A route through a mutual contact does not imply willingness to introduce. Find a two-hop path without inventing a direct edge or getting trapped in a cycle. |
| Recover where we left off | An interaction can involve several people, a source, and commitments with owners and states | A public observation is not a conversation and must not reset a contact cadence. Replay the same meeting twice: one interaction, with all participants preserved and no duplicate promises. |
| Ask who has talked about a topic | Linked source, transcript span and timestamp, speaker attribution if known, and participant roles | Leave full transcripts in the original system or a private document store. Search eligible passages by keywords and meaning, then traverse to the speakers and relevant relationships. Distinguish “discussed it,” “has demonstrated experience,” and “might know someone.” Don’t credit a topic to every attendee. |
| Reconstruct a relationship or role change | Event or valid time, recorded time, source, and corrections | Separate when a change took effect from when you learned it. A later correction should not change what the assistant would have known on the earlier date. Preserve genuine conflicts; don’t guess an exact date from a vague one. |
| Get a nudge when it’s useful | Dated commitments, optional cadences, partial dates, recurring dates, and confirmed public events | Use a separate update queue. An ordinary tool reply can indicate pending updates; the main assistant fetches and acknowledges them separately. Test replay, dismissal, timezone changes, and a birthday stored without a year. Don’t infer emotions or the cause of a relationship change merely from timing. |
| Keep important records current | Opt-in watch configuration, identity evidence, source confidence, and a change receipt | GroktoCrawl is an optional public-evidence source, not the owner of the graph. Compare stable public identifiers where available, deduplicate repeat reports, and send uncertain or conflicting matches to review. Watch only selected people; do not pass private notes or transcripts to the crawler. Validate the available polling/webhook contract before committing to it. |
| Leave or restore the system | Source-scoped identifiers, export versions, relationship history, and document references | Consider CSV/vCard contact export and iCalendar for dates if useful. Neither substitutes for a native export that preserves graph links, time, and provenance. Test a backup restore, import preview and reversible merge; avoid treating shared email or matching names as proof of identity. |

## Architecture questions to resolve before implementation

- **Record and retrieval.** Which questions need typed paths and exact temporal filters, and which need text or semantic retrieval first? Compare a small relational baseline against a dedicated temporal-graph option on the same fixtures. For one user, sparse life events and anniversaries are not the same workload as dense telemetry; a time-series database is not a foregone conclusion.
- **Document ownership.** Which sources remain linked, which need a local archival copy, and under what policy? Plan for changed or deleted source material, invalidated index entries, and access checks on passages, embeddings, and links. Keep private meeting context distinct from facts observed on the public web.
- **MCP contract.** The main agent can send and query information conversationally. Decide how GRM returns stable references, evidence, uncertainty, proposal IDs, and receipts without exposing storage schemas. Do not assume the MCP server can wake any host. The reminder experience must work when the main agent next asks.
- **Curation and authority.** A small decision model can classify observations, suggest entity links, score ambiguity, or rank updates. Durable writes still need validation, replay protection, correction and deletion behavior, and a human-mediated escalation path through the main agent. Do not assume a model-generated confidence score settles identity. One worker or several is deliberately undecided.
- **Single-user operations.** One owner still needs authenticated clients, narrow integration credentials, backups and migration, a clear inference-provider boundary, and export and recovery without losing history. No automatic outreach, multi-user social graph, shared feed, or sales-pipeline scope.

A useful design review should show examples of a direct relation, an indirect path, a missed transcript attribution, a late or conflicting correction, an overdue promise, and an unrelated update queued without disrupting the answer. These are test scenarios, not promises about a particular stack.
